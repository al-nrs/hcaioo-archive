Before You Renew an AI Scribe, Price the Work the Invoice Misses

A renewal worksheet for finance, clinical operations and the teams that keep the service running.

Augmented Leadership HQ2026-11-10

An AI scribe renewal can tell you exactly what the license costs and still leave the CFO unable to answer a basic question. What does the hospital spend to get one eligible visit documented, including when a patient declines recording or the clinician has to finish the note by hand?

A November 2 PLOS Digital Health opinion by Natarajan and colleagues explains how consumption fees, fixed licenses and local hosting put different parts of the bill in different places. It also calls attention to costs the vendor may never invoice, including engineering and oversight. The article offers a way to examine costs. It supplies no comparative hospital cost estimate or measured return. Natarajan et al.

We would make a broader renewal conditional on a local comparison of the full documentation workflow, with an affordable fallback when the tool goes unused. If that comparison is missing, fund a time-limited evaluation or negotiate a narrower renewal with a review date.

Budget for the path patients actually take

Patient choice changes what the hospital has to staff and support. In a separate November 4 study, Stein and colleagues interviewed 20 Patient and Family Advisory Council members across nine hospital sites in one network. Participants valued knowing when AI was involved and wanted clinicians to retain decision authority. The authors propose disclosure, assent and consent as communication approaches scaled to the stakes. The interviews can inform local design. The small advisory sample cannot tell a hospital how often its own patients would decline, and the framework does not establish legal requirements. Stein et al.

For ambient documentation, ask clinical leadership, privacy and patient experience to specify who explains the recording, how the patient's choice is recorded and what happens if the patient declines or withdraws. Have the clinic test the conventional documentation path during an ordinary staffed session. Make sure interpreter-supported visits and technical failures have a working path too. Those steps take staff time, and their feasibility matters to the renewal.

Start with the eligible visit

Pick one clinic and define eligible visits before looking at usage. Specify visit types, scheduled sessions and supported languages. Compare the proposed service with the current documentation process in comparable sessions. Match or stratify by clinician, visit type and language when practical, and record differences in case mix or staffing before interpreting the result.

Keep every eligible completed encounter in the denominator, including those where the patient declines, the tool fails or staff never start it. Count visits once. Track declines and failures separately so the team can distinguish a patient's choice from a reliability problem. Keep a separate count of notes signed by the clinic's existing deadline, including those completed without the tool.

Take this worksheet to the renewal meeting

Use one agreed reporting period for both workflows. Enter the amount or rate, the source, and any missing data for each line.

  1. Vendor and implementation spend. Procurement and IT record license fees, usage and overages, hosting, interfaces and incremental implementation, validation and training costs in dollars. Use invoices, contract schedules and project ledgers. Separate one-time from recurring spend and put both on the same renewal horizon. Ask for the charge rule when one visit generates several billable events, and compare the bill with actual use before committing to a larger minimum.
  2. Documentation work. Clinical informatics and the clinic manager add active person-minutes for notice and questions, troubleshooting, review, correction and conventional documentation. Divide by all eligible completed encounters. Use sampled workflow observation with EHR timestamps for context. Include time after clinic and report minutes by role. The COO can see whether the tool removes work or moves it to another team.
  3. Patient choice and fallback. Patient experience and clinic operations report declines over eligible encounters offered the tool, and visits offered over all eligible encounters. Use the encounter roster and a locally approved choice log. For declines and failures, report notes signed on time over visits in each group. Review why a path failed before expanding. Never treat a patient declining as a staff performance failure.
  4. Quality and ongoing controls. Clinical quality and IT report consequential documentation errors over independently audited signed notes, with counts and missing records for each workflow. Use authorized records and an agreed audit rubric. Put recurring monitoring, support, revalidation and remediation expenses in the budget. Review serious incidents separately from averages and apply existing escalation rules.
  5. Cash and capacity. Finance reports incremental cash spend per eligible completed encounter and the total over the renewal term. Count incremental paid labor once and show existing staff time separately. Translate released time into capacity only if staffing, rooms and demand allow the clinic to use it. Report cash benefit only when payroll or ledger evidence supports lower outlays or finance verifies incremental collected contribution after variable costs.

Ask privacy and procurement to record any proposed vendor reuse of clinical data beside the price sheet. Specify permitted uses, retention and the terms that apply at exit. A lower fee does not price those rights for the hospital.

Make one decision this week

Bring the worksheet and the price schedule to the renewal meeting. Have finance sign off on what the hospital can afford, the clinic manager demonstrate the conventional path and clinical quality state what the audit supports. Give unresolved evidence a named owner and a decision date.

Expand only where the clinic can support both documentation paths, the quality review supports the proposed use and the budget can carry the full cost. If the evidence is incomplete, keep the scope and spending capped while the team closes the specific gap. If staff cannot complete documentation reliably when the tool goes unused, fix that workflow before adding sites.

Reply with the decision on your desk, whether to renew, expand or run a smaller evaluation, and the worksheet line you still cannot fill in.
